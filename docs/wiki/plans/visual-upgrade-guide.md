# Your guide to the Lanternmoss visual upgrade

> **Two files, two audiences:** [visual-upgrade.md](visual-upgrade.md) is the instruction set for AI agents.
> This guide is for you, the product owner: what you do, review and decide during production.
> **The upgrade is still planning-only.** This guide does not start production or approve spending or release.

## What you are making

You are giving Lanternmoss a softer, more expressive look: rounded characters, inviting homes, gentler
lighting and clearer menus. Animal Crossing is a visual reference; the witch, warrior, archer, lanterns,
small planets and action combat keep their own identity.

The agents will prepare the art, connect it to the existing game, and check that movement, fights,
saves and loading still work. You choose the direction, review the playable results and decide what
is ready to share. You do not need to manage the code or produce every piece of art yourself.

Start with one small playable village area. Only expand after you like both how it looks and how it
feels to play. For the full boundaries, see the agent plan's
[style and scope section](visual-upgrade.md#3-what-the-style-change-means).

## Before you start

- [ ] Choose whether to finish your current gameplay milestone first or prioritize the visual sample.
- [ ] Tell the agents which version you want to keep. If you are preserving a finished edition first,
      complete [TODO 43](../../../TODO.md#43-preserve-the-classic-version-before-a-visual-upgrade-core-checkpoint).
      Ask to see that the backup opens and your exported adventure can be restored.
- [ ] Name the devices you care about, such as your laptop and its usual browser. Do not assume phone
      controls or multiplayer are included in this visual update.
- [ ] Set a time/spending limit and say whether supplied, generated or purchased art is acceptable.
- [ ] Authorize a specific starting stage when ready. For example: “Start V01. Prepare the inventory
      and first-release options for my review. Do not begin full art production.”

You can preserve an earlier version without waiting for every optional feature. The recommended first
visual release covers the original three worlds; adding Tidewhisper is a separate choice.
See [timing and preservation](visual-upgrade.md#1-recommendation-and-timing).

## 1. Phase V-A: Scope and direction

**Covers V01–V02.** You choose what changes and what the game should look like.

**What the agents will do:** They will list the characters, scenery and screens that need updating,
then show a few different treatments of the same hero, tree and cottage. They will turn your chosen
examples into a short style guide so later art follows the same direction.

**What you do**

- [ ] Choose which worlds and screens belong in the first release and which can wait.
- [ ] Show references you like and explain what you like: the shapes, colors, expressions or lighting.
      Also name anything you want Lanternmoss to keep.
- [ ] Compare the examples at normal playing size, not only in large close-ups.
- [ ] Decide whether to allow imported art files alongside objects made by the game's code.
- [ ] Choose how the main 3D characters will be made: supplied models, commissioned work, or an agreed
      creation approach. A generated concept image shows a design; it is not yet a moving game character.
- [ ] Approve one direction and keep its reference images for later comparisons.

**Good looks like**

- [ ] The hero, cottage and tree look as though they belong in the same game.
- [ ] You can describe the direction in a few words and explain why you chose it.
- [ ] The result feels like Lanternmoss, with clear limits on what is being changed.

**Your decision:** approve the direction and allowed art sources at **G0**, the style checkpoint.
If the examples are not convincing, ask for another comparison before more art is made.

## 2. Phase V-B: Budgets and foundations

**Covers V03–V06.** You check that the chosen look is practical in the actual game.

**What the agents will do:** They will measure how the game runs, try one scenery object and one moving
character, and compare lighting and camera options. They will prepare a repeatable way to add art
while keeping the existing game and saves working.

**What you do**

- [ ] Confirm the devices, loading expectations and smoothness you want the agents to test.
- [ ] Choose between the lighting/camera examples while moving around, talking and fighting.
- [ ] Review the simplest graphics setting too: can you still see faces, paths, objects and danger?
- [ ] Confirm who provides any missing models and whether outside help fits your limit.
- [ ] Ask for a plain-language summary: what works, what is too costly, and what has not been tested?

**Good looks like**

- [ ] Characters move naturally, objects have sensible sizes and the camera helps you see the action.
- [ ] A fresh launch works without missing art, and switching places does not steadily slow the game.
- [ ] The agents show test results from named devices rather than claiming that one attractive image
      proves the game will run well everywhere.

**Your decision:** at **G1**, the readiness checkpoint, choose whether the evidence is enough to proceed
to the playable sample. A missing required test still needs to be completed before release; your visual
approval does not make an untested result pass.

The game stays browser-based initially. If the agents propose changing that, ask for a small comparison,
its cost and its effect on your existing game before approving a separate project.
See [platform choices](visual-upgrade.md#4-platform-and-project-decision) and
[measurement rules](visual-upgrade.md#6-proposed-budgets-and-measurement-rules).

[Agent plan: Phase V-B](visual-upgrade.md#phase-v-b-budgets-and-foundations)

## 3. Phase V-C: Playable proof

**Covers V07.** You try the whole direction in one small village corner.

**What the agents will do:** They will bring together a witch, villager, companion, cottage, tree,
water edge and a few usable objects in a small playable scene. They will show matching before/after
views and report what making that sample cost in time and effort.

**What you do**

- [ ] Walk, turn, talk, gather, open a chest, enter the cottage and try an attack.
- [ ] Look at the scene by day, at night and in weather, using your usual playing distance.
- [ ] Compare it with the approved style images. Say which parts match and which feel wrong.
- [ ] Check comfort: can you see clearly, follow the camera and play without distracting stutters?
- [ ] Review the revised time/spending estimate before approving larger batches of art.

**Good looks like**

- [ ] You enjoy being in the scene, not just looking at its screenshot.
- [ ] Characters, scenery, lighting and menus feel coherent.
- [ ] The small scene still plays like Lanternmoss and meets the agreed checks.

**Your decision:** at **G2**, approve full conversion, request changes to the sample, or hold the upgrade.
If you dislike the basic proportions or mood, this is the time for a redo; do not multiply that design
across every character and world first.

## 4. Phase V-D: Released-game conversion

**Covers V08–V13.** You review the new look across the original three worlds.

**What the agents will do:** They will update the accepted release in batches: heroes; villagers,
pets and wildlife; outdoors; buildings; enemies and effects; then menus and item art. They will keep
checking that the new appearance has not changed the game's rules or broken existing adventures.

**What you do**

Review each finished batch in play. You do not have to inspect every file or every internal change.

| Batch | Your review |
| --- | --- |
| **V08: Heroes and equipment** | Try all three heroes. Check walking, attacks, tools, hats and capes; compare their menu previews with how they look in play. |
| **V09: Villagers, pets and wildlife** | Check recognizable personalities, natural movement and useful expressions. Friendly creatures should be easy to tell apart from enemies. |
| **V10: Outdoors** | Walk through grass, paths, cliffs and shores. Find resources and judge crop readiness without guessing; check that the visible ground matches where you can walk. |
| **V11: Homes and building** | Enter houses, use furniture and place/remove building pieces. Check doors, room visibility and whether the placement preview matches the finished object. |
| **V12: Enemies and effects** | Fight regular enemies and bosses, including in heavy weather. You should see where an attack will land and when it is safe to respond. |
| **V13: Menus and item art** | Use the bag, shop, crafting, dialogue, journal and settings. Check small text, item recognition and larger text settings; useful information should stay visible. |

**Good looks like**

- [ ] The approved style holds across all three worlds without making them look identical.
- [ ] New art does not make fights confusing, menus harder to read or homes harder to use.
- [ ] Every item promised for this release is accounted for; unfinished art is listed honestly.

**Your decision:** give focused feedback as batches arrive. At **G3**, the original-worlds coverage
checkpoint, decide whether the selected first-release content looks complete. Save a wholesale style
change for a deliberate new decision, rather than asking each batch to follow a different reference.

## 5. Phase V-E: Expansion integration

**Covers V14–V16.** You review later worlds when their gameplay is ready.

**What the agents will do:** They will carry the accepted style into Tidewhisper, caves and Duskhollow,
then rematches and the Lantern Moon. Each area stays unavailable to normal players until its own
gameplay, visual and performance checks are complete.

**What you do**

- [ ] Confirm which expansions are part of the release you are reviewing. You can release the original
      worlds' visual update without waiting for every expansion.
- [ ] Review each world in its intended conditions, using the prompts below.
- [x] Decide on extra additions separately from the required world work: the otter is queued as TODO 27i, with public unlock subject to the release budget.
- [ ] Check that public images and menus do not reveal the secret world early.

**Good looks like**

| World or encounter | Your review |
| --- | --- |
| **V14: Tidewhisper** | You can read the shoreline and understand what changes at low/high tide. Its village, creatures and lighthouse feel coastal; water effects do not hide danger. |
| **V15: Caves and Duskhollow** | Darkness feels atmospheric, but you can still find paths, objects and attacks on simple graphics settings. The alternate beetle appearance is just as clear and usable as the spider appearance. |
| **V16: Rematches and Lantern Moon** | Harder fights show their new attacks clearly. Floating movement, eclipses and the final resolution are understandable, and the Moon keeps its intentionally small scale. |

**Your decision:** approve each world's appearance and inclusion separately. Ask to see the corresponding
gameplay/release results before agreeing that a pretty preview is ready for players.

## 6. Phase V-F: Optimization and delivery

**Covers V17–V18.** You review the complete chosen release and decide whether to share it.

**What the agents will do:** They will check crowded scenes, loading, repeated travel and old saves,
then fix remaining problems and update the screenshots and documentation. They will present the
finished release, test results and any remaining limitations for your review.

**What you do**

- [ ] Play the first hour and a few later fights on the devices you agreed to support.
- [ ] Review the whole chosen release, including menus, homes, weather and simple graphics settings.
- [ ] Ask for confirmation that your preserved edition and separately backed-up saves remain usable.
- [ ] Read the short list of remaining issues. Decide which must be fixed and which can wait.
- [ ] Approve the public screenshots and release description; they should show the actual finished game.
- [ ] Decide whether the temporary old/new comparison view is still needed. That choice is separate
      from keeping your preserved classic copy.

**Good looks like**

- [ ] The release is consistent and pleasant to play, with no unexplained slowdowns or lost progress.
- [ ] All promised content is present and the required checks have passed.
- [ ] You know exactly what is being released and what is still planned.

**Your decision:** at **G4**, the final release checkpoint, approve the selected release or name the
remaining fixes. You do not need to manage version history or the agents' routine code checks yourself.

## Decisions that need you

These are product decisions, not requests to approve every small code change. Tell the agents what
you approve clearly; you do not need to repeat an approval unless the proposal changes.

| When | What needs your decision | What you should see first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Before production** | Start now or finish a gameplay milestone first; preserve which edition; authorize which starting stage | Current status, preservation options and proposed first-release content |
| **G0, after V02** | Choose the style, allowed art sources and first-release content | Comparable hero/tree/cottage examples and a short style guide |
| **G1, after V06** | Proceed to the playable sample; accept the proposed device/loading expectations | Working art examples, camera/lighting choices and an honest test summary |
| **G2, after V07** | Convert more of the game, revise the sample, or hold | A playable village sample, before/after views and an updated effort estimate |
| **G3, after V08–V13** | Accept the original worlds' visual coverage | Finished batches in play and a list of anything still missing |
| **Each expansion** | Accept its appearance and include it in a release | Its playable content and required gameplay/performance evidence |
| **G4, after V17–V18** | Release the agreed content and decide whether the comparison view stays | Final playable result, passing checks, screenshots and remaining-issue list |
| **Whenever the proposal changes** | Approve extra spending, a new style, more content or a move away from the browser project | What changes, why it is needed, cost/time and alternatives |

At these points, you can ask the agents to hold the next product stage while you review. If a decision
is already covered by your clear earlier approval, there is no need for another round of confirmation.
Failed checks need fixes; missing required evidence needs testing. Neither can be replaced by “looks fine to me.”

Useful responses:

- “Approve this direction for the sample only. Keep full conversion on hold.”
- “Approve this batch. Use these images as the reference for the next batch.”
- “Revise the night lighting and show it again before continuing.”
- “Hold expansion art. Finish the original three worlds for this release.”

See the agent plan's [review checkpoints](visual-upgrade.md#10-review-gates-and-future-roadmap-overlap).

## How to give useful feedback

Tell the agents **where it happens, what feels wrong and what you want to preserve**. A screenshot or
short clip helps with visual problems; a few steps help with problems that happen while playing.
You do not need to diagnose the code.

Use this short form:

> **Where:** Witch, village, night, simple graphics setting.<br>
> **Problem:** Her face disappears into shadow while I talk to a villager.<br>
> **Wanted result:** I should still read her expression.<br>
> **Keep:** The warm lantern colors and the cottage design.<br>
> **Next review:** Show the same conversation again after the change.

Choose the level of change you want:

- **Small correction:** “Keep the design. Make the tree less bright and show the same daytime view.”
- **Movement problem:** “When the witch stops walking, her feet slide. Try walking and stopping on
  the path; keep her current shape and colors.”
- **Readability problem:** “The boss glow hides the attack warning. Keep the warning easy to see,
  including on simple graphics settings.”
- **Redo:** “The head and body proportions feel wrong compared with our approved example. Redo this
  character before making the other heroes.”
- **Hold:** “This is a change in direction. Hold the next art batch and show two alternatives first.”

Ask for a redo when the basic design is wrong, the result repeatedly misses the agreed reference,
or a local adjustment cannot solve the problem. Ask for a fix when the design is right but something
is too bright, too small, awkwardly placed or broken in motion.

For lost progress or a game that stops working, stop that test and report what you did just before it
happened. Keep the saved file if you have one, and ask for the agents' test copy rather than repeatedly
overwriting your own adventure.

Approve the changed result after seeing the same situation again. Keep taste feedback separate from
extra feature requests: “Make the cottage rounder” is a visual change; “Add another room” adds gameplay scope.

## What to leave alone while agents are working

You can keep sending references, feedback and priority changes. Coordinate these actions first so you
do not disturb work that is being edited or tested:

- **Do not edit, rename or replace active game/art files yourself.** Send the replacement and explain
  which version you want used; keep reference images in an agreed separate place.
- **Do not switch, reset or delete the agents' working copies.** Ask them to open a separate copy of
  your classic edition if you want to play it.
- **Do not import over your only save, clear browser storage or use the same save location for both
  editions.** Use an exported backup and the separate test browser session or game link provided.
- **Do not start or stop another game server or change test settings during a test run.** Ask for a
  separate play session if you want to review the game while testing is underway.
- **Do not buy or commission a large collection of models or art before the style and small sample are accepted.**
  Have the agents check a representative model against the agreed needs first.
- **Do not approve from a concept picture alone.** Ask to see movement, normal camera distance and
  the simpler graphics setting before accepting a game-ready result.
- **Do not request a wholesale style change halfway through a batch as a small fix.** Ask to hold
  and revise the direction deliberately so you can judge the extra work.

Your job is to keep the direction clear and judge the player experience. The agents handle the
implementation and verification described in [their workflow](visual-upgrade.md#7-verification-and-implementation-workflow-when-activated).

## Your quick review checklist

Before approving a stage, ask:

- [ ] Does it match the direction I approved?
- [ ] Have I seen it in play, in the conditions that matter?
- [ ] Can I still understand characters, objects, menus and danger?
- [ ] Have the required checks passed, with untested devices named honestly?
- [ ] Is the next proposed step within the content and spending I approved?
- [ ] Have I said clearly what is approved and what should wait?

Use the phase names and V-numbers when giving feedback so your comments point to the same work in
both files. For the technical details or exact requirements, follow the relevant agent-plan link above.
